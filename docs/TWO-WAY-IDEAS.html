Two-way rows: working from the page you're on

Today the rows show what points at a page. To change one of those nodes, add a new one, or follow a link onward, you leave the page.

These five ideas let you do it right where you are. The example is a page about one concept you are studying, Spaced repetition. Claims, sources, observations, questions, projects and other concepts all point at it. Every idea works the same for any page, tag or property.

In each drawing, what is new has a soft orange background. Every change gets the faded Undo arrow, as today.

1. Group a row by anything

Where you are: the Spaced repetition page. Nine claims point at it, and you want to see at once which hold up and which are disputed.

  1. Click the small words after the count, "grouped by".
  2. Pick Confidence. You can also pick the tag, the source, the month, or any property the claims have.
  3. The claims show in groups. Click a group's name to fold it. The row remembers your choice.

In your notes: nothing changes. Only how the row is shown.

2. Add a node inside a group, already filled in

Where you are: the same page, claims grouped by Confidence. You have just read something that cuts against the method and want to note it as a disputed claim.

  1. Point at the Disputed group. A + appears at its end.
  2. Click it. A line opens under the group.
  3. Type the claim and press Enter.

In your notes: a new claim about Spaced repetition, with its Confidence already set to Disputed. You typed only its words. Its Source you fill in when you have it.

3. Write into a node without leaving

Where you are: the same page. A claim catches your eye and you want to add a thought to it, not to the concept page.

  1. Click the small arrow before the claim. Its own bullets open under its line.
  2. Click after the last bullet and type.
  3. Click the arrow again to close it. The concept page is as it was.

In your notes: the new bullet is inside the claim, exactly as if you had typed it on the claim's own page.

4. Follow a chain without leaving

Where you are: the Spaced repetition page. A claim says "Cepeda 2006". You want to know what that source is, who wrote it, and what else it claims, without opening three pages.

  1. Click the arrow before the claim. Under its line you see its properties, and its own rows: what points at the claim.
  2. Click the arrow before its Source, Cepeda 2006. The source opens one step deeper: its authors, and every claim it backs.
  3. Keep going as far as the links go. Each step sits under the one before, so you can see the whole path.

In your notes: nothing changes. You read what is already linked, several steps away, on one page.

5. A row for what sits two steps away

Where you are: the Spaced repetition page. No source points at the concept directly. Sources point at claims, and the claims point here. So the page cannot tell you what the evidence rests on.

  1. Right-click the "claims about" row and choose "Add a row through this".
  2. Pick the claims' property to follow: Source.
  3. A new row appears, "sources behind →", listing every source reached through the claims, each saying how many claims it carries. Click a source to see only its claims above.

In your notes: nothing changes. The row is built from links that already exist, and stays on the page.
